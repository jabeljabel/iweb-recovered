Requirements

Preparedness and participation

Seminar participants are expected to prepare themselves each week by reading, taking notes, and coming to an understanding of the texts. A prepared participant will bring to seminar meetings both their own interpretation and questions about the texts. Participants are expected to have a working knowledge of the historical circumstances of the text production. This will require some cursory research. If participants have prepared in this manner, your expected active participation in seminar discussions will be a matter of course. Participants in the seminar are expected to actively participate.

10 —Reading Notes, Summaries, Keywords, Quotations

To aid your preparation for class discussion (and remembering what you read years from now), you will be required to create a single page document, documenting your reading experience. It can take any form you like, including a full blown reaction essay, simple notes, summaries, a long list of keywords, and/or a list of quotations. But the length shall be no more than one page.

These notes will be shared with other students for the purpose of making a communal reference guide. As a result, they should not be considered private.

Presentations

Participants will deliver two 15-minute presentations during the semester. Presentations should focus on at least one of the assigned texts for that week. An effective presentation develops an interpretation of a text within its historical and/or critical contexts (and therefore will require some outside research as well as close readings of the text at hand). On the day of your presentation, you will also be responsible for monitoring the progress of class discussion, raising questions and helping to address questions under discussion. This is your chance to guide class discussion to your interests, to get your questions answered (and complicated, refined, and developed), and to figure out what you think about the text.

Note that each of you will also serve once as a respondent to the main presentation. Your response should last no longer than five minutes.

Email me and your respondent your presentation outline by 5pm on the Wednesday preceding the Thursday class meeting.

OPTION ONE-ers

Final Paper

A final research paper of 20-25 pages, produced on a topic agreed in consultation with me, is due on Monday of exam week.

Please consult MLA Handbook for Writers of Research Papers for style and formatting guidelines. Alternatively you may also use Chicago Style.

We will conduct a mini-conference during the final class session, in which you will give a ten-minute talk (in the form of a conference paper) based on your final essay. This is an occasion for you to practice presenting the public face of your work in progress.

OPTION TWO-ers

3 Position papers

Participants will post 500-word argumnets during the semester to the Angel website or comparable blog. Students should select readings from the list relevant to their on-going studies. Please post polemics by Wednesday evening prior to the meeting for which they are assigned. A successful polemic presents your entire line of clear argumentation on a topic, following the key turns of your logic. Think of these polemics as addressed to the authors of the text we are reading if it helps you to make a stronger argument. Alternatively, you can think of addressing your classmates to convince them of your opinion. Lastly, you might try direct address to me.

Citations should come primarily from the reading of that week.
