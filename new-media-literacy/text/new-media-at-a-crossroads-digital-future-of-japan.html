“New Media at a Crossroads: Digital future of Japan”

Kukhee Choo (Tulane University)

Human perception of new media has gone through an evolutionary process throughout history. Each media that emerged out of communicational necessities, scientific explorations, or business enterprises during the previous centuries provided new visions for the futures to come and also shaped our understanding of past events through 'backshadowing' practices. Knowledge and public awareness were altered with the expansion of media technology, which were further determined by subsequent social changes. Understanding of this development occurred vertically rather than horizontally, and the ambitious theories of post modernism and globalization failed to fully dismantle this limitation. When Western contemporaries imagine the future of digital humanity, Asia, driven by media determinism, conceive of a democratic utopia achieved through the digital media technology. This example is most witnessed in the case of post 3/11 earthquake Japan, which is currently undergoing radical social and political changes. My talk will focus on the emerging discourse surrounding new media in Japan and how these voices imagine Japan's digital future.
