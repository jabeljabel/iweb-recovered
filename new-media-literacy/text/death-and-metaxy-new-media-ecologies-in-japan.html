“Death and Metaxy – New Media Ecologies in Japan”

His research on film and audiovisual culture in East Asia and especially Japan from the 1960s onward focuses on the connection between larger economic, social, and institutional structures and aesthetic modalities. He is especially interested in the dynamics of contemporary media ecologies, and his recent work explores topics such as various modes of reflexivity, 'amateur' production, and the media mix and its relation to the concept of world. His dissertation mapped the structural equivalence of systems of production / dissemination and textual meaning for popular film genres such as Pink Film, Kadokawa Film, and V-Cinema in Japan. His recent publications have examined the role of postcolonial fantasy in Korean "remakes" of Japanese films, the question of categories in a media mix environment, and the history of German sexploitation cinema.

“Death and Metaxy – New Media Ecologies in Japan”

Alex Zahlten (Harvard University)

The promise of new media ecologies is one of possibility and boundlessness. This paper examines new media ecologies in Japan and proposes that one of their functioning principles is in fact finality, though delivered in small portions. This relativized version of finality affects our relationship to fiction, and it becomes most visible in the treatment of death. Introducing the concept of metaxy as an alternative to medium, this paper will look at several cases of staggered death in popular culture in Japan, and attempt to account for the significant shifts in how we engage with the new media ecologies.
