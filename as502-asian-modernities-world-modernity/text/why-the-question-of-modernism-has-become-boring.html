Why the question of Modernism has become boring

Wednesday, February 22, 2012

[name removed] asked with no little amount of disbelief, “how can we write about twentieth century Chinese culture and avoid the question of Modernism?” or something to that effect.

My answer:

First, it is not the case that we should not think the question of the modern and modernism. Clearly, this area of inquiry has been fecund for scholarship in recent years in Asian Studies. However precisely to the degree that it has become a fecund area for intellectual pursuit, we must now ask ourselves, “to what end?” What has been solved by the industry of “Asian Modernities”? Have a clear answers to the questions (when modernity? what is modernity? what is modernism? how do we know?) been elaborated in this vast body of scholarly endeavor? Or are these questions bracketed? Is “the modern” and modernism simply being used as a marketing hook to talk about whatever a scholar wants to actually discuss? So much has been written that at this point it seems likely that assessing whether, why, when or where something is modern or expressing modernist sentiment is likely not to drastically change either our understanding of the cultural object of inquiry nor of our understanding of the terms modernity and modernism.

Which leads me to the following point.
