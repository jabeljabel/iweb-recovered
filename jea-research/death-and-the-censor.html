In the first issue of The Osaka Humor Press (Osaka kokkei shinbun) published in November 1908 after the banning of The Humor Press (Kokkei shinbun), Gaikotsu joked about the "suicide" of the Humor Press. On one page corner, a box of text titled, "Parasitic Rare Words‚" confronted readers with eight humorous aphorisms. The corner was cut on a forty five degree angle, so that half of the words printed were being read from the next page. When readers turned the severed page, they would reveal alternative endings to the same aphorisms which had lay hidden in another box entitled, "Reread the Same Characters," a satire of the censor can be felt.

The palpable play in the final line/s, for instance, reads alternatively "Mr. Humor Press commits suicide" and "Mr. Humor Press does not commit suicide." Certainly the old Humor Press is no more. But the Osaka Humor Press now exists. Self-censorship has taken its suicidal toll, but the satire lives on. Every issue of the Osaka Humor Press fittingly carries the epithet "suicide edition" on its cover page as if to announce the precarious position between life and death inhabited by the paper.

Sporting a socialist credo, “crushing the strong, helping the weak, opposing the bad, we are an ally to the good,” the jesting Humor Press with a circulation as wide as 80,000 mocked politicians, judges, and censors alike.

Banned as many as 20 times variously on the grounds of both obscenity and sedition, the paper had also been the victim of libel cases and fines as great as 200 yen. In its seven year publication run, members of the staff had been arrested five times. After the August 20, 1908 article entitled “There are Many Scoundrels Among the Prosecutors” was banned, the October issue announced the "suicide" of the paper.

In typical sardonic style, editor Miyatake Gaikotsu called the issue the “suicide edition” and included a suicide note. The Humor Press was published no more.
