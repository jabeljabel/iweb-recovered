Rant:

A professor of mine (a close reader himself and probably old enough to have been a contemporary of the New Critics) once told me something like, “the problem with deconstruction is that it produced a handful of top-notch scholars and a slew of third tier scholars. The key to New Criticism was that it could be taught to anyone and you had a few top-notch scholars, a ton of second tier scholars, and an army of third tier scholars, undergrads, and high schoolers doing it.”

To me this idea that “close readings” at the center of the New Critical project were something that has been a leveler (egalitarian) is quite interesting. And now that I’ve heard it, I hear it everywhere. I mean historians talk about doing close readings for crying out loud. Is there no sacred method where only a few bright people with a commitment to the literary can hide from the world?

You can see a version of the notion that the method is exportable at work here: How to do a close reading.

or here: The Art of Close Reading One Two Three

Several questions arise from this sort of notion that the method is infinitely exportable and egalitarian:

Isn’t close reading the dream of education as such? Namely, that anyone (with enough study) can learn to become a good reader, critical thinker, and judge what they read on their own.

Is this why it is being taught to first year grad students?

One problem with the notion of its egalitarianism is precisely the problem or crisis that naysayers of the value of studying literature attack: that you can make it say anything; that it is bullshit; that it is the root of all evil of the contemporary university; that is not scholarly. If anyone can do it, what does expertise entail?

Do you buy it? Is close reading egalitarian or the terrain of elite grad school types?

What are the ethics involved in doing close readings?

What does the method suggest about the text? and texts in general?

What kinds of questions will close readings tend to ignore?

How are the roots of structuralism and semiology related to what we now call close reading?
