Patricia Fister, “Probing the ‘Hidden’ Collections of Japan’s Imperial Convents”

Japan’s imperial Buddhist convents have been referred to as an “endangered species.” Centuries ago when Buddhism was at its height, dozens of such convents were established, but only thirteen remain today. It is a closed world and objects have rarely left the convents’ gates, so virtually nothing exists outside of Japan to inform us about convent culture and history. Even ordinary people in Japan are unaware of the existence of the imperial convents. Yet from the time the religion was introduced, century after century, there were royal and aristocratic women who played key roles in the establishment of Buddhism, the founding of temples and convents, the creation of devotional objects, and the propagation of worship rituals of great beauty and intimacy. This talk will introduce some discoveries that reveal new dimensions about women’s roles in religion and document specific devotional practices and traditions, forcing us to rethink and reconfigure our conception of female Buddhist practice in Asia.
