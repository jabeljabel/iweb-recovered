Sharon Domier, “Collecting China... and Giving It Back”

In his book Japanese War Crimes, Peter Li includes an article by Chinese scholar Zhao Jianmin called “The Looting of Books in Nanjing,” which echoes claims by ‘some scholars’ that “the purpose of book looting was to destroy Chinese culture in order to fulfill the Japanese long term objective of conquering the Asian continent (p.284). There was considerable attention given to book looting in China during the war by the English-speaking library world because of the press campaigns launched by Chinese librarians and American supporters. Books for China campaigns saw thousands of volumes of books sent from the United States to China to help replace some of the losses. Looted books also caught the attention of the occupation officials, who made arrangement for some of the books to be returned to their original owners.  But there is a great more to the story, particularly from the Japanese perspective.

Books such as Nokosareta zosho: Mantetsu Toshokan, kaigai Nihon toshokan no rekishi by Okumura Keiji and Nihongun sesshu tosho: Chugoku senryochi de sesshu shita tosho no yukue by Tomotani Jun’ichi are the products of methodical and thoughtful research using memoirs, memoranda, library records, government documents and interviews. Tomotani’s work in particular cites many JACAR and SCAP documents, so it is much easier to trace primary sources than before. Another Japanese scholar Kanemaru Yuichi is collaborating with Chinese researchers, so hopefully in the next few years there will be a bilateral consensus on the events and outcomes of “book looting” during the Sino-Japanese War.

In this paper, Domier uses these Japanese sources to tell the back story of “The Looting of Books in Nanjing” with vivid descriptions of the mountains of books seized by soldiers, the stress and exhaustion felt by the staff who were selected to sort the materials, such as Yosano Akiko’s son Rin who had a nervous breakdown and after only a few days on the job and Shinjo Shinzo, who died of exhaustion after maintaining a frenetic pace to preserve as many materials as possible under very difficult situations, the contributions to scholarship based on the materials seized in Nanjing, and their eventual fate.
