From Hebdige:

CULTURE

'the best that has been thought and said in the world,'

to

'particular meanings and values' sought to uncover the concealed fundamentals of history; the 'general causes' and broad social 'trends' which - lie behind the manifest appearances of an 'everyday life'.

The implicit assumption that it still required a literary sensibility' to 'read' society with the requisite subtlety, and that the two ideas of culture couId be ultimately reconciled was also, paradoxically, to inform the early work of the French writer, Roland Barthes, though here it found validation in a method-semiotics-a way of reading signs.

in EP Thompson's words, it appeared to reflect the parochial concerns of a group of 'gentlemen amateurs'.

The struggle between different discourses, different definitions and meanings within ideology is therefore always, at the same time, a struggle within signification: a struggle for possession of the sign which extends to even the most mundane areas of everyday life.

Our task becomes, like Barthes', to discern the hidden messages inscribed in code on the glossy surfaces of style, to trace them out

as 'maps of meaning' which obscurely re-present the very contradictions they are designed to resolve or conceal.

As a symbolic violation of the social order, such a movement attracts and will continue to attract attention, to provoke censure and to act, as we shall see, as the fundamental bearer of significance in subculture.
