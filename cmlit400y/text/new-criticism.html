1. as the foregrounding of language

as the integration of language

as fiction

as aesthetic object

as intertextual or self-reflexive construct

Questions:

Can we consider Fowles through Culler? What does it mean to do so? If we applying a Culler’s model of literariness to Fowles, how do we distort the novel? If we maintain the differences of the example (here Fowles’s novel) can we transform Culler’s model? What sort of theory of literature do we find in the novel? Who reads? Who writes? What sort of theories of art and creation are discussed and practiced in the novel? Does the novel adhere to them? What is the novel’s theory? Does it fit with Culler?

Is Culler’s text itself literature? Is it literary without being literature? How can we use his definitions to see if they help us answer these questions? Can we improve on the model?

Does the severing of the the literary object from the author, from practical purposes, serve a particular critical function? When might this function have been useful? What is the global context to Wimsatt writing? How does elaboration of the intentional fallacy impact the relation to the liberal subject as defined by Culler?

One thing we will stress today is the utility of a imagining a continuum between the polar, binary opposites Culler identifies.
