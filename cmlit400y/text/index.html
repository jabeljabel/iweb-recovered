Course Description

What are the underlying structures to our thinking about literary arts? How does understanding the models upon which our interpretations are based help us better understand criticism and the literary object? Why are so many theories unstated?

This course will introduce and examine the most contested field of literary studies, the source of many debates within the humanities, and the butt of far too many jokes in popular media. Although most of us would readily admit that there is likely some model that can ground, order, or explain nearly every phenomenon in the real world including interpretation, explicit articulation of the foundations behind thought about culture has become controversial in recent years.

Although theoretical debates have been part of thinking about literary arts since the earliest records of criticism and interpretation, the efficacy of theory itself has recently become a major component of theoretical and notso theoretical inquiry.

What are the real world implications of theory? Do they matter or is theory a different order of cognition? Is the activity of theory just naval-gazing for insular ivory tower elites or prodding for world transformation and social engagement?

The course will begin with inquiry into the similarities and differences between interpretation, criticism, and critical theory. Issues of application and practice will focus our attention to the formulation of new models for interpretation adn understanding. Topics covered will include: new criticism, narratology, semiotics, author, historicization, Marxism, Feminism, psychoanalysis, reception theory, cultural studies, poststructuralism, and postcolonialism.

We will end with a reflection on the relation of high theory to the culture wars and polarization of the political discourse today.
