DESCRIPTIONS OF COURSE REQUIREMENTS

PARTICIPATION

Class Attendance and Participation 20%

Students are expected to attend all class meetings.  Students are expected to complete all of the reading for the week in time for class.

If a student has extenuating circumstances regarding either of these requirements, that student should contact me immediately.

Regular attendance is required. You are allowed five absences free of consequence. Additional unexcused absences will result in your class participation grade being lowered a letter grade for each additional absence. After nine unexcused absences, you will fail the course. Late work will not be accepted in cases of unexcused absences. You are expected to arrive on time for class; recurrent tardiness may result in a recorded absence. Failing to complete reading or do other course preparation for class meeting will also be recorded as a participation failure for the day.

10 Course Material Write-ups 10%

Students will be expected to complete 10 course material write-ups on primary materials by the end of the semester. The form is available here.

Forms on a given week’s readings should be submitted as a hard copy at the beginning that Wednesday’s class or as digitally via email prior to class on Wednesday. (In other words, students are to have finished the primary reading for the week prior to class on Wednesday.)

The Write-ups are opportunities for you to display what you have garnered from the readings and to influence class discussions. Students who have difficulty speaking in class are particularly encouraged to take this assignment seriously as it will prepare you to be more outspoken in class and show your classmates and instructor that you have important ideas.

Material in the write-ups will be shared with the entire class and students should not consider them confidential work for the eyes of the professor only.

In-class Presentation 10%

The presentation will consist of a small research project and in-class oral/visual presentation.  The purpose of the presentations is to educate the class on some historical, material, financial, geographical, biological, biographical, other contexts to a given week’s text or on topics which may be confusing or which may have been touched on only tangentially in class lectures or readings.   Handouts or slides (consisting of visual aids, quotations, timelines, definitions of terms, and/or annotated bibliographies) are required.  You must submit to professor Abel a copy of your slides and/or handouts.

Presentations should not exceed ten minutes in length. Presentations will generally happen on Monday’s class.

All presenters should plan a meeting with me in office hours to discuss their topic at least one week prior to their presentation. The meeting is to facilitate you arriving at a topic that is both of interest to you and beneficial to the class.

All students must sign up for a week to give a presentation by the third week of class.

WRITTEN WORK :

Special Project 15%

This project is to be decided by the student and approved by professor Abel. The idea behind it is to allow students to think beyond the scope of the traditional research paper. Possible successful projects examples include retelling a fairy tales in graphic novel format, translating scenes from an as yet untranslated graphic novel, reframing stories we have read in class using alternative different images (from your own design or taken from elsewhere), and tellings of new stories using a mash-up of images from graphic novels in our course. But the possibilities are limited only by your imagination and by your ability to convince professor Abel that this is a worthwhile, stimulating, and intellectual endeavor that will get you thinking about graphic novels in a way which you would otherwise have been unable to achieve.

SPECIAL PROJECTS will be presented on Fridays. Sign up for your date by February. Your project must be complete by the day you present it. Make sure to clear your topic with Professor Abel in office hours at least two weeks prior to presenting your special project to the class.

RESEARCH

Students should discuss their topics with me during office hours prior to writing.  Research papers should include a close analysis and interpretation (not mere summary) of at least one literary work discussed in class, a discussion of how the work relates to several of the course readings, and inclusion of sources not used in class.  All papers should be in the MLA (Modern Language Association) style format.  Students are encouraged to use academic journals, books published by university presses, and primary sources as references for their research.

Policies

EVALUATION RUBRIC FOR ATTENDANCE & PARTICIPATION 

90-100 % Student is well prepared, as demonstrated by her/his critical response papers and comments made in  class; is interested in and respects others‘ opinions; asks and responds to questions; demonstrates  knowledge of course materials; fosters a vibrant intellectual community. 

80-89 %  Student is generally prepared, makes an effort to refer to readings and course topics; is willing to  participate in discussions in class. 

70-79 %  Student reluctantly participates in class activities and is often unprepared; rarely practices critical thinking;  does not demonstrate sufficient knowledge of course materials. 

60-69 %  Student is unwilling to participate in class activities and unable to ask or answer questions; is generally  unprepared; demonstrates little knowledge of course materials. 

0-59 %  Student is absent (physically or mentally), unprepared, or disruptive in class.

Academic Integrity    

Dishonesty of any kind will not be tolerated in this course. Dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, cheating, plagiarizing, fabricating information, facilitating acts of academic dishonesty by others, having unauthorized possession of exams, submitting work of another person or work previously used without  informing the instructor, or tampering with the academic work of other students. Students who are found to be dishonest will receive academic sanctions and will be reported to the University‘s Judicial Affairs office for possible further disciplinary sanctions.  Penn State defines academic integrity as the pursuit of scholarly activity in an open, honest and  responsible manner. All students should act with personal integrity, respect other students‘ dignity, rights and property, and help create and maintain an environment in which all can succeed through the fruits of  their efforts (Faculty Senate Policy 49-20).  Possible academic sanctions include reduced course grade to F for course. In addition, there are  disciplinary sanctions that may include the XF grade and permanent expulsion.

During the drop/add period at the beginning of the semester, the Department of Comparative Literature  encourages students to visit all courses in order to make an informative judgment about which ones to  take. After the first week, however, only students registered in the course may remain. Any late-add (or reenrollment of a dropped registration) after the third week of the semester has to be petitioned to the  department on a form available in the office, 427 Burrowes.
