This graduate seminar explores the world of reality fiction.

 

Reality Fiction is literature that foregrounds a general truth about all forms of representation—they can be true without being perfect mirrors of the world.  By directly engaging with questions of realism and the real, Reality Fiction forces us to articulate the odd relations between the written word and the world where those words are read.  Sharing much with its historical successor Reality TV, Reality Fiction has long been a dominant interest in literary criticism and theory under the labels—mimesis, representation, realism, identity fiction, fictional truth, and virtual reality.  In addition to examining these different models for conceiving literature in relation to the real, this course will settle on three global literary forms — autopoesis (first person narrative including confession, testimony, ich roman, or I-novel), the rise and fall of proletarian literature, and the more recent rise of overtly politicized narratives of identity. 

 

Fictional texts may include work by Augustine, Proust, Soseki, Joyce, Borges, Marquez, Mishima, and Lu Xun as well as work to be determined by the literary interests of seminar participants.  Theoretical materials from Plato, Marx, Twain, Lukacs, Genette, Riffaterre, Massumi, Karatani, Derrida, Kant, Lacan, Jameson, Spivak, and Zizek.
