Preparedness and participation

Seminar participants are expected to prepare themselves each week by reading, taking notes, and coming to an understanding of the texts. A prepared participant will bring to seminar meetings both their own interpretation and questions about the texts. Participants are expected to have a working knowledge of the historical circumstances of the text production. This will require some cursory research. If participants have prepared in this manner, your expected active participation in seminar discussions will be a matter of course. Participants in the seminar are expected to actively participate.

 

Presentations

Participants will deliver at least one 15-minute presentation during the semester. Presentations should focus on at least one of the assigned texts for that week. An effective presentation develops an interpretation of a text within its historical and/or critical contexts. On the day of your presentation, you will also be responsible for monitoring the progress of class discussion, raising questions and helping to address questions under discussion.

 

Note that each of you will also serve once as a respondent to the main presentation. Your response should last no longer than five minutes.

 

Email me and your respondent your presentation outline by 5pm on the Wednesday preceding the Thursday class meeting.

 

OPTION ONE-ers

Final Paper

A final research paper of 20-25 pages, produced on a topic agreed in consultation with me, is due on April 30.

 

Please consult MLA Handbook for Writers of Research Papers for style and formatting guidelines. Alternatively you may also use Chicago Style.

 

We will conduct a mini-conference during the final class session, in which you will give a ten-minute talk (in the form of a conference paper) based on your final essay. This is an occasion for you to practice presenting the public face of your work in progress.

 

 

OPTION TWO-ers

2 Summary and 3 Response Posts

Participants will post 500-word summaries during the semester to the Angel website or comparable blog. Students should select readings from the list relevant to their on-going studies. Please post summaries by Wednesday evening prior to the meeting for which they are assigned. A successful summary presents the text’s entire argument or plot, following its key turns. You may be revisiting these texts in the future for your oral examinations, dissertation, teaching, and book projects. Think of these summaries as part of your permanent archive to which you can return to refresh your memory of each text.

Participants will respond to three posts of their choice over the course of the semester.
